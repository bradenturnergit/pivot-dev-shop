Meet Famous People: the party game everyone plays from their own phone.

Three rounds, one deck of famous names, and every phone in the room in the game. Our first product is out now on iPhone and Android.

1 December 2026· 3 minute read· The Famous People team

This post first appeared on the Famous People blog. Famous People is a game we built at Pivot Dev Shop; here’s how it got made.

Every family and friend group has that game. Someone tears up scraps of paper, everyone scribbles famous names, the names go into a hat (or a bowl, or a cereal box), and for the next hour the room is loud. Some people call it the name game. Some call it Celebrity, or just “the hat game.”

We loved it. We didn’t love hunting for pens, deciphering handwriting, arguing about who was keeping score, or watching half the room sit out while one phone got passed around. So we built Famous People: the classic game, where everyone’s in, playing from their own phone.

It’s free, there are no ads, and nobody needs to sign up. It’s out now on iPhone and Android, just in time for the holidays.

How it works

From sofa to showdown in four steps

Someone hosts

Tap Host a game and you get a 4-character code and a QR code.

Everyone joins from their own phone

Scan the code or type it in, add your name, and you’re in. No accounts, no passwords, no app needed for guests; they can play in the browser too.

Fill the deck

Everyone secretly adds famous names: movie stars, singers, athletes, world leaders, your favorite cartoon duck. Type your own or borrow from our category lists.

Pick teams

Drag players into teams or hit Shuffle and let fate decide.

That’s it. Setup takes about as long as deciding who hosts.

The twist

Three rounds. Same deck. Rising chaos.

Here’s the part that makes Famous People different from every other guessing game: every name gets played in all three rounds, and each round takes away a way to describe it.

Round 1: Say it

Describe the person any way you like, but don’t say their name. No spelling it out, no “rhymes with.” Accents and sound effects are encouraged.

Round 2: Act it

Charades, in total silence.

Round 3: One word

You get one word. Say it once. Then stop.

By round 3, you’re not really describing famous people anymore. You’re describing what your uncle said about them in round 1. That’s where the in-jokes come from, and why every game ends with people quoting each other for weeks.

Your turn

60 seconds, all eyes on you

When it’s your turn, your phone buzzes and lights up. Tap to start the clock, then get your team to guess as many names as you can in 60 seconds. Tap each correct guess to bank the point. Everyone else’s phone shows whose turn it is and the time left, so the whole room watches the countdown together.

No skipping. If time runs out, the name goes back into the deck. Scores update on every phone instantly, so nobody has to do math at a party.

Why we made it

Everyone’s in

Most party apps are built for one phone passed around the room. That’s fine for two minutes, but at a real gathering it means most people are watching, not playing.

Famous People turns every phone in the room into a controller. Everyone’s in. Grandma can play. The cousin who never joins in can play. And if someone doesn’t have a phone, the host adds them and they take their turn on the host’s device.
