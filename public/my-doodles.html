Solution · My Doodles

A sketchbook that hangs itself on the wall.

Snap a page, drop the photo in a shared folder, and a few hours later it's found, straightened and hanging in a gallery — with a link to share any one page. Here's why it exists and how it works.

8 November 2026· 4 minute read· Braden Turner, Pivot Dev Shop

39 finished pages, every one of them living in a camera roll between a grocery list and a parking spot.

Someone in our house fills sketchbooks: dense, patterned, hours-per-page work in marker and gel pen. Every finished page got photographed. And then it disappeared, because a camera roll is where photos go to be scrolled past.

The photos weren't good to look at, either — a page shot from above at a slight angle, sideways, with the spiral binding and half the desk in frame. What the pages deserved was a wall: every page, straight and upright, newest first, nothing else in the picture.

The problem

Why a camera roll isn't a gallery

Buried in everything else

A page from months ago sits between screenshots and receipts. Nobody scrolls back that far, so the old pages stop being seen at all.

A photo of paper

Tilted, sideways, desk in the corner, spiral rings along one edge. The drawing is in there, but it's not what you see first.

Sharing one means texting one

Send a photo to family and that's all they get — one picture in a thread, with no way to see the rest of the sketchbook.

The solution

What My Doodles does

My Doodles is a web gallery that fills itself. The only job left for a person is taking the photo.

Take the photo, drop it in a folder

New photos go into a shared OneDrive folder from a phone. We tried an iCloud shared album first, but Apple now asks every visitor for an email and a code before showing one, which no automatic process can answer.

The page finds itself

Every 4 hours the gallery checks the folder. For each new photo it finds the page's four corners, flattens the angle out, stands it upright and trims the edge. It can tell a sketchbook page from a loose sheet by looking for the evenly spaced row of spiral rings — and when it finds punch holes instead, it trims those too.

A black wall, newest first

Each page hangs in its own shape on black, with nothing competing with the colour. Tap one and it opens alone, full screen; swipe for the next, pull down to go back to the wall.

Share one page — and the rest come with it

The share button sends a link to just that page. The message shows the drawing itself as its preview, the link opens straight onto it, and closing it leaves the person on the wall with every other page.

A small admin page for the rest

Sign in to hide a page, delete one (with undo), or fix a crop by dragging four dots onto the page's corners, with a magnifier under your finger. "Publish now" runs the import straight away instead of waiting for the next check.

How it got built

From a shared folder to a gallery in a day

My Doodles started with an approved design for the wall and a folder of photos. Claude Code built everything behind it: the page-finding, the automatic import, the admin page and the sharing — each change reviewed and previewed before it went live.

The decisions worth explaining

Automatic first, a person second. The import got all four corners exactly where a person put them on 24 of 39 pages, and told sketchbook pages from loose sheets on all 39. The near-misses aren't worth hours of tuning: the admin page flags the ones it's unsure of, and a fix is four dots and a tap.

Unlisted, not locked. Anyone with the link can look, so family needs no account; search engines are asked to stay away. Only the owners can sign in to change anything.

No app store. It's a web page that adds to the home screen with its own icon and opens full screen, so a change is live everywhere in a minute.

A sketchbook deserves a better home than the camera roll.
